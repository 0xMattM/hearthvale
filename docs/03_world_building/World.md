# Purpose

The World System defines the shared environment where all players interact, gather resources, hunt animals, fight monsters, trade, and compete for territory.

The world exists to connect players.

Private lands are where players produce.

The world is where players interact.

---

# Vision

The world should always feel alive.

Players should constantly encounter other players exploring, hunting, gathering, traveling, or preparing for war.

Unlike player lands, the world belongs to everyone.

It is a shared space where opportunities and dangers coexist.

---

# Design Goals

The World System must:

- Encourage exploration.
- Promote player interaction.
- Support the economy.
- Provide combat content.
- Create strategic locations.
- Feel alive without relying on scripted events.

---

# Functional Requirements

### WORLD-001

The game contains one persistent shared world.

---

### WORLD-002

All players exist in the same world.

---

### WORLD-003

The world contains public areas that cannot be owned.

---

### WORLD-004

Players can freely travel between public areas and private lands.

---

### WORLD-005

Animals and monsters spawn only in public areas.

---

### WORLD-006

Strategic territories exist for guild competition.

---

# World Philosophy

The game world is not where players build their economy.

The game world is where players discover opportunities.

Production happens primarily on player lands.

Exploration happens in the shared world.

---

# World Structure

The world is divided into three major environments.

## Town

The central social hub.

Contains:

- Marketplace
- NPCs
- Tutorials
- Starter equipment
- Community spaces

The town is always a safe zone.

---

## Public Wilderness

Open areas where players gather resources and hunt.

Examples:

- Forests
- Mountains
- Plains
- Swamps
- Rivers
- Lakes

These locations regenerate naturally over time.

No player owns them.

---

## Strategic Territories

Special public regions that can be controlled by guilds.

These regions provide economic bonuses but never replace player-owned lands.

Territory ownership is temporary.

---

# Player Lands

Player lands are not physically part of the shared map.

Instead, they are accessed through travel.

Each land is an instanced area owned by one player.

Players may visit other players' lands if permissions allow.

---

# Safe Zones

Safe zones include:

- Town
- Player Lands

Combat between players is disabled.

These areas focus on production and social interaction.

---

# Dangerous Zones

Dangerous zones include:

- Monster Areas
- High-Level Hunting Areas
- Territory War Regions

Players should prepare before entering.

Danger creates value.

---

# Exploration

Exploration should reward curiosity.

Possible rewards include:

- Rare monsters.
- Better hunting locations.
- Unique gathering areas.
- Future events.

Exploration should generate opportunities rather than mandatory progression.

---

# Travel

Players travel freely between locations.

Future transportation methods may include:

- Horses
- Wagons
- Boats
- Fast Travel Points

Travel should remain meaningful without becoming tedious.

---

# Biomes

Different regions specialize in different resources.

Examples:

Forest

- Trees
- Deer
- Mushrooms

Mountains

- Stone
- Iron
- Wolves

Swamp

- Herbs
- Rare plants
- Poison monsters

Plains

- Wild animals
- Farming resources

Biomes encourage exploration and regional diversity.

---

# Resource Regeneration

All public resources regenerate naturally.

Players never permanently deplete the world.

The limiting factor remains player energy and production capacity.

---

# Animals

Wild animals roam naturally throughout appropriate biomes.

Animals are not owned by players.

Their populations regenerate continuously.

---

# Monsters

Monsters inhabit dangerous regions.

Higher-risk regions generally contain stronger enemies and more valuable materials.

Monsters should support the economy by providing unique crafting resources.

---

# Dynamic Population

Future versions may include:

- Animal migration.
- Seasonal spawning.
- Regional population changes.
- Dynamic world events.

These systems should make the world feel alive.

---

# Relationship with Lands

Player lands provide safe production.

The world provides exploration and danger.

Both systems complement each other.

Neither replaces the other.

---

# Relationship with Economy

The world introduces resources into the economy.

Player lands transform those resources into greater value.

The Marketplace connects both.

---

# Relationship with Combat

Most combat occurs within the public world.

Players leave the safety of their lands to hunt, explore, and gather valuable resources.

---

# Relationship with Guilds

Guilds compete for control of strategic territories.

Territory control never affects ownership of personal lands.

Guild conflict exists only in designated public regions.

---

# Anti Pay-to-Win

Owning NFT lands does not grant control over the public world.

Every player has equal access to exploration, hunting, and public gathering areas.

Success depends on preparation, strategy, and cooperation.

---

# Risks

## Risk

The world feels empty.

### Mitigation

Encourage player movement through resource diversity, events, and exploration.

---

## Risk

Players remain only on their lands.

### Mitigation

Place exclusive resources, monsters, and guild objectives in public areas.

---

## Risk

One region becomes the only valuable destination.

### Mitigation

Distribute valuable opportunities across multiple biomes and territories.

---

# Future Expansion Ideas

Potential additions include:

- Seasonal weather.
- Dynamic events.
- World bosses.
- Hidden caves.
- Ancient ruins.
- Ocean exploration.
- Treasure hunting.
- Traveling merchants.

# Open Questions

Should regions have different climates?

Should weather influence gathering efficiency?

Should day and night affect monster behavior?

Should players discover hidden locations?

Should some resources appear only during specific seasons?

---

# Final Statement

The world is the shared stage where every player meets, explores, hunts, and competes.

Player lands provide ownership and production.

The public world provides discovery, risk, and opportunity.

Together they create a living sandbox where exploration supports the economy and every journey has the potential to generate value.
