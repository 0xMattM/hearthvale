# Resources

**Document Version:** 0.1.0
**Status:** Draft
**Owner:** Game Design Team
**Last Updated:** July 30, 2026

---

# Purpose

The Resource System defines every raw material and processed material that circulates within the game economy.

Resources are the foundation of all production chains.

Every profession depends on resources.

Every crafted item originates from one or more resources.

The economy exists because resources continuously move between players.

---

# Vision

Resources should never feel like meaningless collectibles.

Every resource should have value.

Every resource should have demand.

Every resource should participate in multiple production chains.

Players should constantly ask:

> "Should I sell this resource, process it, or keep it for later?"

That decision is the heart of the economy.

---

# Design Goals

The Resource System must:

- Drive the player economy.
- Encourage specialization.
- Create supply and demand.
- Connect professions.
- Support long production chains.
- Keep old resources relevant.

---

# Functional Requirements

### RES-001

Every resource must be produced through gameplay.

Resources are never generated from nothing.

---

### RES-002

Every resource must have at least one consumer.

---

### RES-003

Every resource must belong to one or more production chains.

---

### RES-004

Every resource must eventually leave the economy.

Resources should be consumed.

Permanent accumulation causes inflation.

---

### RES-005

Resources should have multiple uses whenever possible.

---

### RES-006

Resources should remain valuable throughout the lifetime of the game.

---

# Resource Categories

Resources are divided into six major categories.

---

## Natural Resources

Obtained directly from nature.

Examples

- Wood Logs
- Stone
- Iron Ore
- Copper Ore
- Clay
- Sand
- Water

---

## Agricultural Resources

Produced through farming.

Examples

- Wheat
- Corn
- Potatoes
- Cotton
- Herbs
- Vegetables

---

## Animal Resources

Produced by animals or hunting.

Examples

- Leather
- Meat
- Milk
- Eggs
- Wool
- Feathers

---

## Monster Resources

Obtained through combat.

Examples

- Monster Hide
- Monster Claws
- Horns
- Bones
- Venom

Monster resources should primarily support advanced crafting.

---

## Processed Materials

Created by transforming raw resources.

Examples

- Wooden Planks
- Iron Bars
- Steel Bars
- Flour
- Fabric
- Leather Sheets

These materials create value by requiring additional professions.

---

## Manufactured Goods

Final products.

Examples

- Furniture
- Weapons
- Tools
- Armor
- Food
- Construction Materials

Future versions may introduce:

- Common
- Fine
- Superior
- Masterwork

- Warehouses
- Guild Storage
- Land Storage
- Specialized Containers

# Economic Principles

Resources create value through scarcity of time.

Not scarcity of availability.

Natural resources regenerate.

The limiting factors are:

- Energy
- Profession Level
- Land Capacity
- Time
- Production Decisions

---

# Relationship with Lands

Every resource originates from one of two places:

Player Lands

or

Public World Areas

Player Lands

Examples

- Crops
- Trees
- Farm Animals
- Production Stations

Public Areas

Examples

- Wild Animals
- Monsters
- Exploration Resources

This balance encourages players to alternate between private production and world exploration.

---

# Relationship with Professions

Every resource belongs to at least one profession.

Examples

Iron Ore

Primary Producer

Miner

Consumers

Blacksmith

Builder

Tool Maker (future)

No resource should belong to only one profession whenever possible.

---

# Relationship with Crafting

Crafting transforms resources into higher-value products.

Resources become more valuable when combined.

The game should reward processing rather than selling raw materials whenever appropriate.

---

# Relationship with Marketplace

The marketplace determines resource value.

Developers do not define prices.

Supply and demand determine prices naturally.

Different regions may eventually create different market opportunities.

---

# Inflation Control

The game must continuously remove resources through:

- Crafting
- Construction
- Cooking
- Repairs
- Consumables
- Future mechanics

Without sufficient sinks, inflation becomes unavoidable.

---

# Anti-Exploitation

Resources should not be infinitely generated without cost.

Production always requires one or more of the following:

- Energy
- Time
- Space
- Tools
- Profession Progression

These constraints protect the economy.

---

# Risks

## Risk

One resource becomes useless.

### Mitigation

Every resource must participate in multiple recipes and production chains.

---

## Risk

Players hoard resources.

### Mitigation

Introduce continuous resource consumption through crafting, repairs, food, and future systems.

---

## Risk

Market oversupply.

### Mitigation

Allow prices to fall naturally.

Players should adapt by producing different goods.

The economy should self-correct through supply and demand.

---

## Risk

Too many resource types overwhelm players.

### Mitigation

Introduce resources gradually.

Organize them into clear categories.

Maintain intuitive naming.

# Final Statement

Resources are the bloodstream of the game's economy.

Every profession creates them.

Every crafting recipe consumes them.

Every marketplace transaction moves them.

Every player's decisions influence their value.

A healthy Resource System creates a healthy economy, encourages player interaction, and ensures that production remains meaningful throughout the lifetime of the game.
