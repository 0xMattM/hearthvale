# Purpose

The Crafting System transforms raw resources into materials, equipment, consumables, construction components, and other valuable goods.

Crafting is one of the primary value-creation systems in the game.

Players increase the value of resources by combining knowledge, profession progression, tools, energy, and production stations.

The system connects gathering professions with manufacturing professions, creating continuous economic interaction between players.

---

# Vision

Crafting should feel like operating a real production chain rather than simply clicking a recipe.

Each crafted item represents the work of multiple professions.

A sword is not simply crafted.

It begins as ore extracted by a miner.

The ore becomes bars through blacksmithing.

Wood becomes a handle through carpentry.

Both materials are assembled into the final weapon.

The economy should reward collaboration, specialization, and planning.

---

# Design Goals

The Crafting System must:

- Increase the value of resources.
- Connect professions.
- Encourage specialization.
- Create long production chains.
- Generate continuous market demand.
- Reward planning instead of repetition.

# Crafting Philosophy

Crafting is not intended to skip gathering.

Crafting exists to increase value.

Every additional production step should increase:

- Economic value
- Production complexity
- Player specialization
- Market opportunities

---

# Crafting Pipeline

Every recipe belongs to a production chain.

General flow:

Natural Resource

↓

Processed Material

↓

Component

↓

Finished Product

↓

Player Use

↓

Consumption

↓

New Demand

The economy remains healthy because resources continuously re-enter the production cycle.

---

# Recipe Structure

Every recipe defines:

- Required resources
- Required quantities
- Required profession
- Minimum profession level
- Required production station
- Energy cost
- Crafting time
- Output items
- Experience rewards

Example:

Iron Sword

Requirements:

- 3 Iron Bars
- 1 Wooden Handle

Profession:

Blacksmith

Station:

Forge

Output:

Iron Sword

# Production Stations

Crafting usually requires specialized stations.

Examples:

- Furnace
- Forge
- Carpenter Bench
- Kitchen
- Loom
- Alchemy Table

Stations are built on player lands.

Public stations may exist in towns for beginners, but private stations provide greater convenience and progression opportunities.

# Crafting Progression

As profession levels increase, players unlock:

- New recipes
- Faster crafting
- Reduced energy costs
- Improved yields
- More efficient production chains

Higher progression should improve efficiency rather than invalidate previous recipes.

---

# Relationship with Resources

Crafting transforms resources into more valuable products.

Raw resources should almost never represent the highest-value economic output.

The economy should reward processing.

---

↓

---

# Relationship with Lands

Crafting stations occupy space on player lands.

Players must decide:

- Build another forge?
- Expand farming?
- Increase storage?
- Add animal pens?

Land management becomes part of the production strategy.

---

# Relationship with Energy

Crafting consumes energy because it represents productive work.

More advanced recipes generally require greater effort.

Profession progression may improve energy efficiency over time.

---

# Economic Philosophy

Crafting should increase value.

Not simply duplicate resources.

The value of a crafted item comes from:

- Resources
- Time
- Energy
- Profession Level
- Production Space
- Knowledge

These factors create meaningful prices within the marketplace.

---

# Anti-Inflation

Crafting removes resources from circulation.

Every recipe acts as a resource sink.

This continuous consumption helps stabilize the economy.

---

# Anti Pay-to-Win

Crafting progression cannot be purchased.

Players improve through experience and production.

NFT lands may improve production opportunities.

They never bypass profession progression.

---

# Risks

## Risk

Crafting becomes repetitive.

### Mitigation

Encourage long production chains.

Offer diverse recipes.

Provide meaningful economic choices.

---

## Risk

Players only sell raw resources.

### Mitigation

Crafted goods should generally provide greater economic value.

---

## Risk

One recipe dominates the market.

### Mitigation

Ensure every profession produces multiple useful products.

Encourage shifting supply and demand.

---

## Risk

Production chains become too complex.

### Mitigation

Introduce recipes gradually.

Maintain intuitive progression.

Avoid unnecessary intermediate materials.

# Final Statement

The Crafting System is the engine that transforms production into economic value.

It connects every profession, gives purpose to resources, creates demand across the marketplace, and drives long-term player interaction.

A successful crafting system ensures that no profession exists in isolation and that every crafted item represents the combined effort of multiple players across the game's economy.
