# Purpose

Define the minimum narrative frame that supports the sandbox without forcing a linear story RPG.

Lore explains **why the world rewards production, trade, and specialization**. It does not replace the player-driven economy.

---

# Narrative Pillars

1. **Work builds the world** — civilizations rise through crafts and cooperation.
2. **Players write the economy** — no NPC empire sets prices.
3. **Danger exists at the edges** — public wilds threaten; personal lands are sanctuaries.
4. **Ownership is stewardship** — land is opportunity and responsibility, not a weapon.

---

# High Concept Setting

A persistent frontier world recovering from scarcity. Settlements thrive when specialists exchange goods. Guilds later contest strategic public regions; personal homesteads remain inviolate.

The tone is grounded fantasy-industrial: tools, farms, workshops, caravans—not cosmic savior plots.

# Factions (Sketches)

| Faction | Motivation | Gameplay link |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Homestead League | Protect starter rights, fair trade | Early quests / reputation |
| Craft Guilds | Standards, recipes, workshops | Profession identity |
| Frontier Companies | Risk expeditions for rare mats | Exploration / combat mats |
| Territorial Guilds | Control public resource zones | Post-MVP territory wars |

Factions guide flavor and optional reputation—not mandatory class locks.

---

# Player Story Promise

The player’s story is economic: first harvest, first trade, first workshop upgrade, first reputation in a craft, first guild venture.

Scripted quests teach systems; they do not define the endgame.

---

# Content Rules

- Lore must not invent P2W or NPC-dominated markets.
- Named places should map to biomes / regions in world docs.
- Mythology stays light until MVP economy is proven.
