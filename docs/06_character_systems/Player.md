# Purpose

The Player System defines everything related to the playable character.

A player controls a single persistent character that progresses over time through professions, exploration, production, combat, and economic activities.

The character is the player's identity within the world.

Progress should always feel meaningful and permanent.

---

# Vision

The player is not a hero chosen by destiny.

The player is an ordinary person entering a living world.

Their reputation, wealth, knowledge, and influence are built through their own decisions.

The game should never force a specific playstyle.

Players define their own goals.

---

# Design Goals

The Player System must:

- Support multiple playstyles.
- Encourage long-term progression.
- Reward specialization.
- Allow experimentation.
- Preserve player identity.
- Avoid class restrictions.
- Encourage interaction with the economy.

---

# Functional Requirements

### PLAYER-001

Each account controls exactly one character.

---

### PLAYER-002

Characters are persistent.

Progress is permanent.

---

### PLAYER-003

Players cannot create multiple characters on the same account.

---

### PLAYER-004

Characters gain experience through gameplay.

---

### PLAYER-005

Players may learn every profession.

---

### PLAYER-006

Profession progression is independent.

---

### PLAYER-007

Every character permanently owns one Starter Land.

---

### PLAYER-008

Characters may own multiple NFT Lands.

---

# Character Identity

Each player has:

- Character Name
- Appearance
- Character Level
- Energy
- Profession Levels
- Inventory
- Wallet
- Lands
- Guild (optional)

Identity should become valuable over time.

---

# Character Progression

The player progresses in two independent ways.

## Character Level

Represents overall experience.

Obtained from nearly every productive activity.

Examples:

- Harvesting
- Mining
- Crafting
- Hunting
- Building
- Cooking
- Trading (future)

Character Level unlocks long-term progression systems.

---

## Profession Levels

Every profession has its own experience.

Examples:

Mining Experience

↓

Higher Mining Level

↓

Better Mining Efficiency

Character Level and Profession Levels are completely independent.

---

# Character Attributes

The first version of the game intentionally avoids traditional RPG attributes.

There are no:

- Strength
- Dexterity
- Intelligence
- Vitality
- Luck

Player progression is determined by:

- Knowledge
- Profession Levels
- Equipment
- Production Choices
- Economic Decisions

This keeps the game focused on economy rather than character statistics.

---

# Inventory

Every player owns a personal inventory.

The inventory stores:

- Resources
- Materials
- Equipment
- Food
- Crafting Components
- Consumables

Future versions may introduce inventory expansion.

Inventory management should never become frustrating.

---

# Equipment

Players may equip tools required for different professions.

Examples:

- Axe
- Pickaxe
- Hammer
- Fishing Rod
- Sword
- Hoe
- Scythe

Equipment improves efficiency.

Equipment does not define character classes.

Players may freely switch equipment.

---

# Experience Sources

Character Experience may be earned from:

- Gathering
- Crafting
- Building
- Cooking
- Hunting
- Exploration
- Completing future objectives

Every productive action should contribute to progression.

---

# Death

Death should create tension.

Not frustration.

The player should never lose permanent progression.

Possible penalties include:

- Temporary durability loss
- Travel time
- Energy loss

Permanent item loss should be avoided unless explicitly designed for specific content.

---

# Respawn

After death the player respawns at:

- Starter Land
- Owned Land
- Future Spawn Point

Respawn mechanics should minimize frustration.

---

# Player Freedom

Players decide:

- Which profession to train.
- Which resources to produce.
- Which lands to visit.
- Which tools to craft.
- Which markets to supply.
- Whether to play alone or in a guild.

The game provides opportunities.

The player chooses the path.

---

# Multiplayer Identity

Characters become known through their actions.

Possible reputation examples:

- Famous Blacksmith
- Wealthy Merchant
- Top Farmer
- Guild Leader
- Legendary Hunter

Reputation should emerge naturally.

Not through predefined titles.

---

# Relationship with Other Systems

The Player System connects directly with:

- Energy
- Lands
- Economy
- Marketplace
- Crafting
- Combat
- Guilds
- Blockchain

Nearly every gameplay system depends on the player.

---

# Anti Pay-to-Win

Player power should come from:

- Time
- Knowledge
- Planning
- Specialization

Never from purchasing NFTs.

NFT ownership expands opportunities.

It does not replace player skill.

---

# Risks

## Risk

Players create multiple accounts.

### Mitigation

Design the economy so that managing multiple accounts provides diminishing returns through energy limits and profession progression.

---

## Risk

Players max every profession.

### Mitigation

Possible future soft caps.

Increasing specialization rewards.

Profession efficiency scaling.

---

## Risk

Players ignore professions.

### Mitigation

Every valuable item should originate from a profession.

Progression should reinforce specialization.

---

# Future Expansion Ideas

Potential future additions include:

- Character customization.
- Cosmetic equipment.
- Achievements.
- Reputation System.
- Mounts.
- Pets.
- Player housing cosmetics.
- Titles.

---

# Final Statement

The Player System is designed to support freedom rather than restriction.

The player is never defined by a class.

Instead, the player's identity emerges naturally through professions, economic decisions, specialization, reputation, and interaction with the world.

The character becomes a reflection of the player's own journey rather than a predefined role.
