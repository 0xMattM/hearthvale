# Purpose

The Profession System defines how players create value within the game's economy.

Every profession represents an economic specialization rather than a combat class.

Professions determine what players produce, how efficiently they produce it, and how they participate in the player-driven marketplace.

The goal is not to force specialization.

The goal is to make specialization naturally rewarding.

---

# Vision

A profession is not a job assigned by the game.

It is a career chosen by the player.

Players are free to learn every profession.

However, players who dedicate time to mastering a specific profession should become significantly more efficient than generalists.

The economy should naturally encourage specialization without imposing artificial restrictions.

---

# Design Goals

The Profession System must:

- Drive the economy.
- Create interdependence between players.
- Reward long-term progression.
- Encourage specialization.
- Support multiple playstyles.
- Produce meaningful items.
- Generate continuous market demand.

# Profession Progression

Every profession follows the same progression model.

Perform Activity

↓

Gain Profession Experience

↓

Increase Profession Level

↓

Improve Efficiency

↓

Unlock Better Recipes

↓

Produce Higher Value Goods

Progression should feel continuous rather than divided into rigid milestones.

---

# Character Level vs Profession Level

Character Level represents overall experience.

Profession Level represents expertise in one specific field.

Example

A Level 50 character may still be:

Mining Level 12

Cooking Level 68

Blacksmith Level 5

Every progression path is independent.

---

# Profession Experience

Profession Experience should always be earned through gameplay.

Examples

Mining

- Mining ore

Farming

- Harvesting crops

Cooking

- Preparing meals

Blacksmithing

- Forging equipment

Building

- Constructing structures

Experience should never be granted simply by waiting.

---

# Specialization

The game intentionally rewards specialization.

Examples include:

- Faster production
- Better yields
- Lower energy consumption
- Higher quality outputs
- Access to advanced recipes

The objective is to create economic diversity.

Not mandatory class systems.

---

# Profession Efficiency

Higher profession levels improve productivity.

Possible improvements include:

- Faster actions
- Lower energy costs
- Better yields
- Improved crafting success
- Reduced production time

Exact formulas will be documented separately.

---

# Economic Philosophy

Professions create value.

They do not consume content.

A successful economy depends on players producing goods that other players genuinely need.

Every profession should have customers.

---

# Freedom of Choice

Players are free to:

- Focus on one profession.
- Balance several professions.
- Change priorities.
- Experiment.

No irreversible decisions should exist.

---

# Relationship with Lands

Every profession can be practiced on every land.

However, land bonuses encourage players to choose locations strategically.

Example

Agricultural Land

↓

Farmer becomes more efficient.

Mining Land

↓

Miner becomes more efficient.

This creates player movement throughout the world.

---

# Relationship with Energy

Every productive profession consumes energy.

Different activities may consume different amounts.

Energy limits production.

Profession level improves efficiency.

The balance between both systems regulates the economy.

---

# Relationship with Equipment

Examples

Forester

Hunter

Better tools increase efficiency but never replace profession progression.

---

# Anti Pay-to-Win

Profession mastery cannot be purchased.

Players must actively perform profession activities to improve.

NFT ownership may improve production opportunities.

It never replaces experience.

---

# Risks

## Risk

Players master every profession.

### Mitigation

Increasing progression requirements.

Efficiency scaling.

Large recipe trees.

Time investment.

---

## Risk

One profession dominates the economy.

### Mitigation

Strong production chains.

Multiple resource dependencies.

Player-driven prices.

Changing market demand.

---

## Risk

Some professions become irrelevant.

### Mitigation

Every profession must participate in multiple production chains.

No profession should produce only one valuable item.

# Final Statement

Professions are the foundation of the game's economy.

Every item, building, tool, and production chain originates from one or more professions.

The Profession System exists to transform player time, knowledge, and specialization into economic value.

A healthy Profession System creates a healthy economy.
